# Examining Encrypted TLS Application Data

This guide covers identifying the transition point from an open handshake negotiation to a fully encrypted data session.

## What You Will Do
* Locate the packet that activates traffic encryption.
* Examine how data payloads appear after encryption is established.
* Compare secure traffic properties against plain-text HTTP streams.

## Steps

1. **Locate the Security Activation Packet**
   * Keep your packet stream filtered with the `tls` keyword.
   * Scan the list for rows containing `Change Cipher Spec` or `Application Data` (like Packet **923**).
   * Click on the row to view its structural details.

2. **Inspect the Encrypted Payload**
   * Move down to the Packet Details pane and expand the **Transport Layer Security** block.
   * Note how individual data fields are hidden from view.

## What the Image Shows

By inspecting your selected row (**Packet 923**), you verified these secure connection properties:

* **The Security Transition:** The packet contains a `Change Cipher Spec Protocol`, which tells both systems that every packet sent after this moment will be fully encrypted.
* **Encrypted Verification:** Underneath the protocol layer, the details show `TLSv1.2 Record Layer: Handshake Protocol: Encrypted Handshake Message`. Unlike your previous plain-text HTTP captures, there are no readable website headers or strings.
* **HTTP Comparison:** In your earlier plain-text HTTP labs, you could easily read paths like `/success.txt` and text like `Host: example.com`. In this secure TLS stream, the payload is locked inside a generic cryptographic envelope (`Application Data`), making it impossible for outside snoopers to read your web activity.
